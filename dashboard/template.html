Explosive synchronization on sparse networks, and what it says about flash crashes

One research repository, three questions. How does the abrupt collapse of synchrony in adaptive (herding) Kuramoto oscillators move when every unit has only k neighbours? Does the same early warning work before abrupt transitions in every kind of oscillator? And does synchrony in real prices warn of crashes? This page is the entry point: what the project is, how the work was done, every result with its numbers, what could be published, and every page and document of the repository.

What this is

The model is a population of phase oscillators whose coupling strength grows with how synchronised each unit's own neighbourhood already is. That feedback (herding) makes synchronisation explosive: the synchronised state survives down to a coupling λb (the fold) and then collapses at once, with a bistable window between the fold and the coupling at which an incoherent start synchronises. In a market reading, the oscillators are traders or stocks and the collapse of a synchronised state is a crash.

dθi/dt = ωi + (λ/⟨k⟩) rip Σj Aij sin(θj − θi),    ri = |Σj Aij eiθj| / ki
θi, ωi
phase and natural frequency of unit i; frequencies follow a Lorentzian of half-width γ = 0.5
λ, p
coupling strength and herding exponent; the gain rip is the local order parameter of i's neighbours (Zhang, Boccaletti, Guan and Liu, PRL 2015)
k, Aij
number of neighbours and the network: each unit listens to k others, on directed graphs (no back-reaction) or undirected ones
R
global order parameter |mean of eiθ|: 0 is incoherent, 1 is fully synchronised
λb
fold: the lowest coupling at which the synchronised state survives. In the all-to-all limit it is known exactly (Zou and Wang, PRE 2020)

The central question: at finite k the fold moves to λb(k) = λb(∞)(1 + a/k). Three theories predict the coefficient a. The Rician closure (the project's first theory) treats neighbour scatter as frozen. The dynamic theory lets that scatter rotate and filters it, and applies to directed graphs. Dynamic plus reaction adds the echo a unit receives through neighbours that listen back, and applies to undirected graphs. The same expansion predicts how far the synchronised branch R shifts at any coupling above the fold, which can be measured more precisely than the fold itself.

How it was done

The work went through a fixed sequence, and each step had to pass before the next one was trusted.

  1. Audit of the earlier claims

    An external audit found headline numbers that no committed file backed. They were withdrawn or marked, and the key measurement was re-run (docs/CLAIMS_AUDIT.md, docs/CORRECTIONS.md).

  2. A tested package

    src/esfc: graphs, frequencies, a compiled integrator held to the original reference code at 1e-12, the fold estimator (bisection on whether a synchronised start survives), the theory, and run directories that store config, seeds and environment for every result.

  3. Theory

    The leading 1/k expansion with frozen and rotating neighbour scatter, the reaction term for undirected graphs, and the same expansion on the synchronised branch (docs/THEORY.md).

  4. First measurements on the workstation and a cloud machine

    Pilot folds, the directed-graph test that excluded the closure, the oscillator-family studies, and the market tests with a design written before the run.

  5. GPU path for the cluster

    Every simulation was ported to the GPU (many realisations stacked as one block-diagonal graph; one CUDA block per oscillator run) and checked against the CPU code before any result was used: identical without noise, and the same statistics with noise. One GTX 1080 Ti did 12 CPU core-hours of fold measurements in 6 minutes.

  6. Production runs on the Ada cluster

    Branch curve, reciprocity, bistable window, graph structure, fold to k = 96, and the oscillator scans (populations to N = 25600, noise, ramp speed, sparse networks), on up to four GPUs at a time (docs/ADA_RUN_2026-10-24.md).

  7. Analysis, with every number generated from the result files

    The results pages, this hub and the run note are rebuilt from results/ and oscillators/data/. Errors found on the way were fixed, not hidden: four are listed under each result where they changed a conclusion.

Results: sparse networks

Every point is a mean over independent graphs and frequency draws (seeds), with a Student-t 95% interval. Coupling is written as a multiple of the all-to-all fold, and the branch shift as c = k (R − R0), where R0 is the all-to-all synchrony on the same frequencies.

Results: oscillator families

The same questions asked of eleven oscillator models: harmonic, Stuart-Landau, van der Pol, FitzHugh-Nagumo, Kuramoto (with and without inertia, with and without herding), Winfree, Rössler and theta neurons, plus a non-oscillating reference.

Results: markets

AUC: probability that the indicator is higher before a crash onset than at a comparison time. "Unmatched" compares with all other times; "volatility-matched" compares with times of equal volatility, which isolates synchrony beyond volatility (the registered primary hypothesis). Intervals are bootstrap 95%; "rejected" is after Benjamini-Hochberg correction.

How this works

1. Every node is a trading desk, and its whole state is one angle

A desk i has a phase θi, its position in a buy/sell cycle. Its net order flow is the projection qi = sin θi. So θ = +90° is peak buying, θ = −90° is peak selling, and θ = 0° or 180° means flat. The market-wide imbalance that moves the price is the average, Q = ⟨sin θi⟩.

This is why colour is the honest readout: a red node is selling right now, a cyan node is buying. A calm market looks dark because most desks sit near q = 0 and cancel each other out.

2. The equation, term by term

dθi = [ ωi + (λ/⟨k⟩)·ri·Σj Aij sin(θj−θi) + ε·si·sin(−π/2 − θi) ] dt + σ dWi + σc dWc
ωi
Its own pace. How fast this desk cycles if it ignored everyone. Economically this is the inverse trading horizon: a high-frequency market maker has large |ω|, a pension fund has small |ω|. The spread of ω across desks is strategy diversity, and it is the only thing resisting synchronisation. Drawn from a Gaussian with mean exactly zero.
coupling
Imitation. Each neighbour j that desk i watches pulls θi toward θj. λ is how hard desks copy; Aij is the observation network; ⟨k⟩ normalises so the λ axis is comparable across networks. ri is the herding gain: local agreement among i's neighbours, 0 if they are split and 1 if unanimous. That factor is what makes the transition explosive, because a desk copies harder when the desks it watches already agree, which is positive feedback.
ε·si
The shock. A common sell signal pushing the selected desks (si = 1) toward θ = −90°. This is the "sell shock" button. ε is its strength, φ is what fraction of desks it reaches.
σ dWi
Idiosyncratic news. Independent random kicks, different for every desk. This is what keeps a calm market disordered, and also what can randomly trigger a crash when λ sits inside the bistable window.
σc dWc
Common market mode. The same random kick applied to every desk at once. Set it above zero and watch: individual assets start co-moving, because each one loads on the shared shock with weight cos θi, and those weights align exactly when the phases align. With σc = 0 the model produces literally zero return correlation no matter how synchronised it is, which is a defect this term fixes.

3. The one inequality that decides everything

A desk either locks to the crowd or drifts at its own pace. It locks when the pull it feels beats its own detuning:

lock  ⇔  |ωi − Ω|  ≤  hi,    where   hi = (λ/⟨k⟩)·αi·ki·ri

Click any node to see both sides of this inequality computed with that desk's real numbers. Three things follow immediately, and you can watch all three:

  • Hubs lock first. hi is proportional to ki, so a desk with 22 neighbours feels roughly ten times the pull of one with 2. That is the whole reason hub-targeted shocks work with so few desks hit.
  • Slow desks lock first. Small |ωi| means a small left-hand side. Extreme-horizon desks are the last to join and the first to leave.
  • Locking is self-reinforcing. Once neighbours agree, ri rises, which raises hi, which locks more desks, which raises r again. That runaway is the cascade.

5. Every slider, and what physically changes

sliderrangewhat moving it does
λ herding coupling0 to 8 How hard desks copy the desks they watch. The master control. Below 3.1 a crash cannot persist. Between 3.1 and 4.1 the market is bistable and a shock decides the outcome. Above 4.1 it crashes unprompted. Drag it slowly across 4.1 and watch R jump rather than glide.
σ idiosyncratic news0 to 1.2 Independent random kicks, a different one per desk. Raising it blurs the transition and lets noise alone trigger a crash inside the window. Push it high and synchrony is destroyed outright: disagreement beats imitation.
σc common market mode0 to 0.8 One shared random kick hitting every desk at once. It barely changes whether a crash happens, but it controls whether the crash is visible as correlated returns. At σc = 0 the model's assets have literally zero pairwise correlation at any level of synchrony, which is the defect this parameter exists to fix.
f herding fraction0 to 1 What share of desks use the agreement-amplified coupling ri rather than plain imitation. Set it to 0 and the crash mechanism disappears: you get the ordinary Kuramoto model with a smooth transition and no hysteresis. This is the cleanest way to see that herding, not the network, is what makes the transition explosive.
shock ε0 to 3 How hard the sell shock pushes the desks it hits toward θ = −90°. Sets the strength of the trigger, not whether it sticks; persistence is decided by λ.
shock reach φ2% to 100% What fraction of desks the shock touches. Inside the bistable window, 10 to 15% is usually enough to flip the whole market, and far fewer if you aim at hubs.
halt length τ0.5 to 20 How long trading is suspended when you press the halt button. Short halts fail: the herd has not dephased yet and re-crashes on resumption. There is a minimum safe duration that grows with λ and shrinks with the spread of trading horizons.
speed0.25× to 16× How much simulated time passes per frame. Use 0.25× to watch a cascade propagate desk by desk; use 8× or 16× to reach a steady state quickly. It changes only the viewing rate, never the physics.

6. Every button

buttonkeywhat it does
⏸ pause / ▶ runspace Freezes the integration. Rendering continues, so you can pause mid-cascade and click nodes to inspect them without the state moving under you.
⚡ sell shocks The lightning bolt means an exogenous shock, news arriving from outside the market. This one fires a common sell signal at a randomly chosen φ fraction of desks for 8 time units, then switches off. Whether the market heals or stays crashed afterwards is the whole point: it depends on λ, not on the shock.
⚡ shock hubs The same shock and the same ε and φ, but aimed at the highest-degree desks instead of random ones. Because the pull a desk feels scales with its degree, this is far more efficient: hubs lock first and then recruit everyone else. Run both at φ = 0.08 and compare.
⛔ trading halth A circuit breaker. Severs all coupling for τ time units: halted desks neither see others nor are seen, so each free-runs at its own ω and the cluster dephases. Halted nodes get a violet outline and the canvas dims. This is the only control that fights a crash rather than causing one.
↗ auto ramp λ Sweeps λ from 0 up to 8 and back down, plotting R against λ in the panel. The two curves do not retrace. The market crashes on the way up at 4.1 but does not recover until 3.1 on the way down. That loop is hysteresis, the reason a crash outlives its trigger.
⛓ network Switches between ER ⟨k⟩=12 (uniform connectivity, the configuration whose bistable window the research measured) and BA scale-free (has real hubs, but almost no bistable window). Use ER for hysteresis, BA for the hub-targeting story.
◙ colour Cycles three ways. order flow: red/cyan by qi, the default. phase θ: a cyclic map, so you see waves travel even when nothing is crashing. cascade trail: nodes flare bright the moment they lock and fade over time, which makes the propagation front visible sweeping outward. Pair that one with 0.25× speed.
↻ resetr New random phases and a new seed, keeping your current slider settings. Clears the history, the price peak and the hysteresis loop.

Clicking any node opens the desk inspector in the right column: its degree, its own frequency, its phase, its order flow, its local agreement, the field it feels, and the locking inequality worked out with its own numbers.

7. Quick reference: what each control changes

λ
Herding strength. Below λb = 3.1 the market cannot stay crashed. Between 3.1 and λf = 4.1 it is bistable: calm and crashed are both stable, and a shock decides which one you get. Above 4.1 it crashes on its own.
σ
Idiosyncratic news. Raising it smears the transition and lets noise alone trigger crashes inside the window. Very high σ destroys synchrony entirely.
σc
Common market mode. Does not change whether a crash happens; changes whether the crash is visible in return correlations.
f
Fraction of desks using the herding gain ri. At f = 0 you get the plain Kuramoto model and a smooth, second-order transition with no hysteresis. Turn f down and watch the bistable window close.
ε, φ
Shock strength and reach. Inside the window, hitting only 10 to 15% of desks is enough to flip the market permanently.
τ
Halt length. During a halt the coupling is severed, so desks free-run at their own ω and the cluster dephases at a rate set by the spread of ω. Too short a halt and the market re-crashes the moment trading resumes.
speed
0.25× to 16×. Use 0.25× with the cascade colour mode to watch the front propagate desk by desk.

8. Reading the side panels

R and Q
R is synchrony, the length of the average phase vector, 0 = disordered and 1 = everyone identical. Q is net order flow. A crash is R high and Q strongly negative. R high with Q positive is a melt-up, which this model produces just as readily.
Phase circle
Every desk plotted at its angle, radius set by its local agreement. Watch it go from an even ring (disordered) to a tight clump near SELL. The white arrow is the order parameter R·eiΨ: its length is R and its direction is the herd's phase.
R vs λ
Press auto ramp. λ sweeps up (cyan) then back down (red). The two curves do not retrace: the market crashes at 4.1 but does not recover until 3.1. That gap is hysteresis, and it is why a crash can persist long after its trigger is gone.
Phase timeline
The regime history, colour-coded, so you can see the sequence calm → cascade → locked → halt → recovery at a glance.

9. Three things to try

  1. See the cascade. Set speed 0.25×, colour mode to cascade trail, λ to 3.5, then fire a hub shock. Freshly locked desks glow bright and fade, so the propagation front is visible sweeping out from the hubs.
  2. See hysteresis. Press auto ramp and watch the R vs λ panel trace a loop.
  3. See why circuit breakers are hard. Crash the market, then halt with τ = 1. It re-crashes. Halt with τ = 8 and it does not. The minimum safe halt grows with λ and shrinks with strategy diversity.

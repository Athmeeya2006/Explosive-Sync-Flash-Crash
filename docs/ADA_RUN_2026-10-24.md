## 2. Main project (Paper A)

### Reciprocity, `results/paperA_reciprocity_gpu/` (10 seeds; replaces the interrupted CPU run of 2026-10-14)

Branch shift c = k (R - R0) at 1.25 times the all-to-all fold, p = 1, against the fraction rho of mutual links:

| k, N | rho = 0 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24, 20000 | -0.298 [-0.328, -0.268] | -0.389 | -0.523 | -0.635 | -0.795 [-0.843, -0.747] |
| 48, 50000 | -0.295 [-0.320, -0.271] | -0.384 | -0.517 | -0.605 | -0.722 [-0.741, -0.702] |
| theory (dynamic + rho x reaction) | -0.266 | -0.367 | -0.468 | -0.569 | -0.670 |

c changes smoothly and nearly linearly with rho, as the theory implies if the reaction comes from mutual
links only. At rho = 1 the gap to theory halves from k = 24 to k = 48. Figure: `research/figures/p3_reciprocity.png`.

### Branch curve, `results/paperA_branch_curve_gpu/` (10 seeds, k = 12, 24, 48, five couplings, p = 1 and 2)

Extrapolated by c(k) = c_inf + b/k (weighted least squares, `esfc.report.branch_curve_extrapolation`);
ratio c_inf / theory:

| graph | p | 1.1 | 1.25 | 1.5 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| directed | 1 | 0.99 | 1.04 | 1.00 | 1.09 | 1.21 |
| directed | 2 | 1.08 | 1.04 | 1.07 | 1.12 | 0.97 |
| undirected | 1 | 0.88 (2 k) | 0.93 | 0.95 | 0.99 | 1.03 |
| undirected | 2 | 0.72 (2 k) | 0.92 | 0.95 | 0.99 | 0.99 |

- The Rician closure is excluded in every cell, in opposite directions on the two graph types.
- Undirected graphs approach the reaction-corrected theory as k grows at every coupling. Away from the
  fold (2 and 3 times it) the extrapolation agrees to 1 to 3%. Near the fold it falls 5 to 9% short
  (z = 2.4 to 3.4); the predicted shift diverges at the fold, where the 1/k expansion converges slowest, so a
  straight line in 1/k through k <= 48 is least safe there. The A4 run (k = 96, 192) tests this region.
- Directed graphs: the raw values lie about 10% beyond the dynamic theory at every k; after extrapolation
  the mean excess is 6%, with 8 of 10 cells within about 2 standard errors and one clear outlier

  coupling, pooled -0.270 +/- 0.009) should be qualified: agreement to within about 5 to 10%.
- Left out: undirected k = 12 at 1.1 times the fold, below the realised graphs' own fold (fold ratios 1.09
  at p = 1 and 1.14 at p = 2), where seeds left the branch.

Figure: `research/figures/p4_branch_curve.png`.

### Clustering and degree heterogeneity, `results/paperA_structure_gpu/` (10 seeds, N = 10000)

Fold coefficient k (ratio - 1), 95% interval:

| graph | clustering at k = 12 (generator sample, N = 2000; not recorded by the run) | p = 1, k = 12 | p = 1, k = 24 | p = 2, k = 12 | p = 2, k = 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| random regular (0 triangles) | 0.005 | 1.07 [1.01, 1.13] | 1.21 [1.12, 1.30] | 1.66 [1.59, 1.73] | 1.88 [1.72, 2.04] |
| 3 triangles per node | 0.050 | 1.09 [1.03, 1.15] | 1.23 [1.11, 1.36] | 1.80 [1.74, 1.86] | 1.86 [1.79, 1.94] |
| 6 triangles per node | 0.096 | 1.20 [1.11, 1.28] | 1.29 [1.16, 1.42] | 1.93 [1.85, 2.01] | 1.96 [1.85, 2.08] |
| Erdos-Renyi | | 0.34 [0.26, 0.42] | 0.79 [0.71, 0.87] | 1.10 [1.00, 1.21] | 1.63 [1.46, 1.80] |

- Clustering raises the fold modestly (at k = 12, clustering 0.1 acts like about 11% fewer neighbours).
- Degree heterogeneity lowers it strongly: with the 1/<k> normalisation, high-degree nodes are coupled more
  strongly and hold the synchronised state. Neither effect is in the leading-order theory (THEORY.md section 5);
  a heterogeneous mean field (Restrepo, Ott, Hunt 2005) is the obvious extension.
- The random regular control reproduces the A1 pilot at k = 24 (1.0505 against 1.0504) on independent seeds.
- Theory for the heterogeneity effect (docs/THEORY.md section 7, `esfc.hetero`): the degree-based mean field gives
  lam_b / lam_b(inf) = 1 - (p+2)/(2(p+1)^2) CV^2 exactly at leading order (derived symbolically, checked numerically
  to 1e-7), so a_het = -3/8 (p = 1) and -2/9 (p = 2) for Poisson degrees. Random-regular coefficient plus a_het
  matches the Erdos-Renyi measurement at k = 24 (p = 1: 0.82 against 0.79 [0.71, 0.87]; p = 2: 1.64 against
  1.63 [1.46, 1.80]) but not at k = 12 (0.66 against 0.34; 1.40 against 1.10). `paperA_hetero_gpu` (k = 24, 48,
  96) is queued to test whether the two add at leading order.

### Bistable window at low connectivity, `results/paperA_window_gpu/` (8 seeds, N = 10000, k = 3 to 24)

Fold (synchronised start) and forward threshold (random start, waiting 400 per coupling) per realisation:

- The closure's critical connectivity is not seen. At p = 1 it predicts a width below 0.007 for k <= 6; the
  measured undirected width is 0.04 to 0.11 there (interval above zero in 3 of 4 cells). At p = 2 it closes the
  window at k = 3 to 4; measured 0.09 to 0.15. Bistability survives down to k = 3.
- Above that, the closure's width is close at p = 1 (measured / predicted 0.90 to 1.06 for k >= 10; 1.57 at k = 8)
  and too large at p = 2. The forward threshold at p = 1, k >= 10 follows the closure's instability of the
  incoherent state (k = 24: 1.59 against 1.66).
- The relative width barely depends on reciprocity (directed and undirected differ by a median 6% for k >= 8)
  although the edges themselves differ greatly (k = 3, p = 1: undirected fold 2.52, directed 0.97 times the
  all-to-all fold).
- On directed graphs the fold lies below the all-to-all fold at small k (p = 1: k <= 8; p = 2: k <= 4), independent
  of N (p = 1, k = 4: 0.925, 0.938, 0.942 at N = 3000, 10000, 30000). The directed fold is therefore not monotonic in
  k (below 1 at small k, about 1.016 near k = 12 to 24, then 1 + 0.54/k), which needs a large negative higher-order term.
- Reciprocity rescales the coupling: the undirected/directed ratio is the same for the fold and the forward
  threshold (p = 1, k = 4 to 24: within about 1%, except 2.3% at k = 6; k = 3: 8%), while the ratio itself ranges
  from 1.04 to 1.64. Mutual links act like lambda divided by f(k), moving both edges together, which is why the
  relative width hardly depends on reciprocity. At p = 2 approximate (up to 5%). Leading order f(k) = 1 + reaction/k
  (p = 1: 1.047 predicted at k = 24, 1.040 measured).
- Provisional until the robustness runs (N = 3000 to 30000; waiting time 1600) agree, because the forward
  threshold is a first passage. Page section P6, figure `research/figures/p6_window.png`.

## 3. Oscillator studies

- **A9** (Z1 for the four herding scenarios): all 48 runs usable in every scenario; on `oscillators/index.html`.
  It breaks the Z1 headline: before the FitzHugh-Nagumo herding collapse the collective variance falls, not
  rises (AUC 0.03 against the strict null; 0.00 at N = 1600 in A1), so "collective variance warned in every
  transition" no longer holds. The page text is now generated from the AUC table and says so; the summary,
  the publishable list and `oscillators/README.md` were corrected. Van der Pol and Roessler herding and
  Kuramoto with inertia warn (AUC 1.00). Why (from the stored example run): 84% of the power of R in the
  FitzHugh-Nagumo herding run sits at the spiking period (36.6 time units), because the phase is event based;
  the detrended variance is mostly this ripple, and the ripple shrinks as the units drift apart (detrended
  variance 4.4e-3 down to 2.1e-3 across the segment). Averaged over one period, R's slow variance in that run
  is not monotone (3.4e-5, 7.0e-5, 1.35e-4, 5.2e-5, 3.7e-5 over fifths of the segment). Settled by a rerun of
  the scenario with 100 runs (`z1_fhn_period.py`, `data/z1_fhn_period.csv`): averaged over one spike period
  (36.5 time units), the collective variance warns (AUC 0.86 against the strict null, raw 0.02), as do the
  AR(2) rate (0.85, raw 0.20) and the spectral peak (0.71, raw 0.10). The slow mode is present; the spike ripple
  hides it. The failure is also noise dependent: at sigma = 0.05 the raw collective variance warns (A2: 0.96 to
  0.99), at sigma = 0.1 it fails at every N tried (400 to 6400).
- **A1** so far (N = 1600, 100 runs, GPU): the collective-variance AUC moves by at most 0.03 downward relative
  to N = 400 (Stuart-Landau onset 0.74 to 0.73, theta neurons 0.94 to 0.92) and rises elsewhere (van der Pol
  onset 0.93 to 0.99); the FitzHugh-Nagumo herding failure persists (0.03 to 0.00).
- **A10** (O5, frequency-degree correlation on a Barabasi-Albert network): largest hysteresis gap, correlated
  against shuffled frequencies: Kuramoto 0.82 / 0.10, Stuart-Landau 0.79 / 0.19, van der Pol 0.87 / 0.43,
  Roessler 0.78 / 0.68, FitzHugh-Nagumo 0.14 / 0.13. The correlation makes synchronisation explosive for
  Kuramoto, Stuart-Landau and van der Pol; Roessler is just as hysteretic without it (areas 0.007 and 0.006),
  and FitzHugh-Nagumo shows no hysteresis either way. Roessler was first swept from coupling 0.08: the
  shuffled down sweep diverged at 0.0727 on a hub of degree 81, at the same coupling for dt = 0.01 and
  0.0025, because lam k = 5.9 is beyond the upper edge (about 4.6) of the stability window of x-coupled
  Roessler oscillators (Pecora and Carroll 1998). It was rerun on the workstation with the top at 0.045,
  inside the window for every node (`o5_frequency_degree.py --only rossler`). The summary step, which had
  crashed on the diverged run, now reports valid bins per sweep.
- **A11** (O9, propagation on Winfree and theta-neuron networks): measured where a stable steady state exists
  (Winfree on SF, theta neurons on ER); the other two configurations have none and are excluded by design.
- **A12** (O3e): the exact mean-field self-consistency puts the Stuart-Landau herding fold at 0.455 (p = 1),
  against 0.468 measured and 0.520 from phase reduction: amplitude dynamics account for the 10% gap.
- **A8** (Z2 ramps at three noise levels, N = 100 to 1600, ramp times 30000 and 100000): at sigma = 0.1 the
  escape is at the predicted fold (within 0.1%); at sigma = 0.3 and 0.5 it is 1.3% and 4.0% below it, the same
  at N = 400 and 1600 and at both ramp speeds. The prediction treats noise as an extra width gamma + sigma^2/2
  in the Ott-Antonsen reduction, exact only for Cauchy noise; with Gaussian noise the fold is lower. The
  variance ratio before escape rises with noise: about 2.0, 2.4 and 2.9.
- **A3** (Z2 ramps, sigma = 0.1, ramp time 3000, N = 100 to 819200, 16 runs each): the escape lag past the
  fold shrinks from 1.34% at N = 100 to 0.75% by N = 6400 and stays at 0.75% (standard error about 0.003%) up
  to N = 819200, so above N about 6400 the remaining lag is the deterministic slow-passage delay of the ramp.
  The variance ratio near/far rises to about 2 up to N = 1600 and then falls to 0.31 at N = 819200, but
  N * var_far is constant (about 0.08) only up to N about 3200, and var_far stays at about 6e-6 from N = 25600
  on: the ratio at large N is an artifact of an N-independent floor in var_far. Suspected cause: the +-60
  clip on the Lorentzian frequency quantiles, which puts 0.53% of the units at exactly +-60 (two
  identical-frequency clusters that start in phase). A rerun without the clip (Z2_CLIP=inf,
  `oscillators/ada/z2_noclip.sbatch`, N = 400 to 819200) is queued as job 38512.
  **Resolved (job 38512, 2026-10-25):** without the clip, N * var_far is 0.070 at every N from 400 to 819200
  (0.0699, 0.0691, 0.0696, 0.0692, 0.0700, 0.0696, 0.0701), var_far falls as 1/N down to 8.6e-8, and the
  near/far variance ratio rises monotonically from 1.96 (N = 400) to 2.52 (N = 819200) instead of collapsing.
  The escape lag is unchanged (0.754% at N = 819200, standard error 0.002%). So the floor was the two
  identical-frequency clusters at +-60; the clip does not move the fold, only the variance at large N.
  Caveat for Z1: oscillators/zoo.py lorentz_q clips at 60 too, so Lorentzian Z1 variance indicators at
  N >= 25600 carry the same floor; that makes their warnings conservative (it can only flatten the rise).
